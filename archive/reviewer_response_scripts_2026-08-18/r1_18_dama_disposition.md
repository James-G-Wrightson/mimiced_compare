# R1.18 -- Discharge against medical advice and other presentations that left before completing care

Generated by `r1_18_dama_disposition.R` from `ANALYSIS/ed_single.parquet` (n = 201,106 first ED visits per patient). All verification checks passed.

## Verdict

DAMA is directly identifiable in MIMIC-IV-ED and these presentations **were included** in the analysis sample: the `disposition` field records 756 visits (0.38% of the 201,106 analysed) as LEFT AGAINST MEDICAL ADVICE, alongside 2,597 LEFT WITHOUT BEING SEEN and 1,913 ELOPED, giving 5,266 visits (2.62%) that ended before care was completed.
None were excluded by any of the three algorithms -- the Resources rule excludes only ADMITTED, TRANSFER and EXPIRED, so every incomplete-care visit remained eligible to be labelled potentially avoidable, and 4,121 of them (8.6% of the 48,181-visit Resources group) were so labelled.
The mechanism is the one the reviewer suspects: Resources flags a visit when no cardiac rhythm was charted and no medication was dispensed, and patients who leave before treatment satisfy both conditions by construction -- 98.0% of LEFT WITHOUT BEING SEEN visits have neither recorded versus 35.6% of completed HOME discharges, so 98.0% of LWBS visits are flagged as potentially avoidable against 35.6% of HOME visits.
Lower urgency does not explain it: the incomplete-care group has a triage distribution essentially identical to HOME (mean ESI 2.88 vs 2.84, median 3 in both), while DAMA patients are *more* urgent (mean ESI 2.45, 50.1% triaged ESI 1-2 vs 25.6%), older and more comorbid (mean age 50.3 vs 43.1 years, SMD +0.39; Charlson >= 1 in 20.0% vs 9.7%, SMD +0.29) -- yet 43.5% of DAMA visits are still flagged by Resources.
Within every triage stratum the incomplete-care groups are flagged far more often than HOME patients of the same acuity (at ESI 3: LWBS 98.2% and ELOPED 67.2% vs HOME 35.0%), confirming that the Resources flag is partly measuring *non-receipt of care* rather than *low resource need* -- a genuine construct-validity weakness, not merely a labelling quibble.
The effect on the headline is real but bounded: making these visits ineligible reduces Resources from 48,181 (23.96%) to 44,060 (21.91%) and the "flagged by at least one algorithm" total from 100,568 (50.01%) to 99,081 (49.27%), a fall of 1,487 visits -- so the substantive conclusions survive, but the paper's description of the Resources group as patients "discharged home" is inaccurate and should be corrected.

## C. The "left before completing care" (LBCC) group

LBCC = LEFT WITHOUT BEING SEEN + ELOPED + LEFT AGAINST MEDICAL ADVICE = **5,266 visits (2.62% of the sample)**. Of these, 4,121 are flagged by Resources, which is **8.55% of the 48,181-visit Resources group**. Adding the 818 flagged `OTHER` visits gives 4,939 (10.25%) of the Resources group that were not discharged home after a completed visit.

The medication field is the discriminating one. 98.1% of LWBS visits have no medication dispensed against 36.1% of HOME visits (SMD +1.76), and 98.0% have neither field recorded against 35.6% of HOME visits. ELOPED sits between the two (65.1% both missing). DAMA is the mildest case (43.5% both missing) because these patients were usually registered, triaged and partly worked up before leaving -- yet that still exceeds HOME by 7.9 percentage points.

The rhythm criterion contributes almost nothing on its own: rhythm is unrecorded in 98.6% of HOME visits and 94.2% of ADMITTED visits, so it separates neither minor from major presentations nor treated from untreated ones. Effectively, Resources = "no medication dispensed".

## E. Impact on the Resources estimate

Two ways of removing the dependency. Scenario 2 adds the three LBCC dispositions to the Resources exclusion list, leaving the sample and the other two algorithms untouched -- the like-for-like sensitivity analysis. Scenario 3 removes LBCC visits from the sample altogether, which changes every denominator.

| Scenario | Denominator | Diagnosis (NYU) | Resources | Triage-Acuity | Flagged by >=1 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Published analysis | 201,106 | 68,173 (33.90%) | 48,181 (23.96%) | 15,716 (7.81%) | 100,568 (50.01%) |
| LBCC ineligible for Resources (sample unchanged) | 201,106 | 68,173 (33.90%) | 44,060 (21.91%) | 15,716 (7.81%) | 99,081 (49.27%) |
| LBCC removed from the sample entirely | 195,840 | 65,194 (33.29%) | 44,060 (22.50%) | 15,094 (7.71%) | 95,898 (48.97%) |

- Resources falls from **48,181 (23.96%)** to **44,060 (21.91%)**, a reduction of 4,121 visits (8.6% relative).
- The headline "flagged by at least one algorithm" falls from **100,568 (50.01%)** to **99,081 (49.27%)**, a reduction of only 1,487 visits, because 2,634 of the 4,121 LBCC visits flagged by Resources are also flagged by the Diagnosis or Triage-Acuity algorithm and remain in the total; 1,487 were flagged by Resources alone.
- Under the stricter scenario 3 the sample becomes 195,840 visits, Resources becomes 44,060 (22.50%) and "at least one" becomes 95,898 (48.97%).

So about one in twelve Resources-flagged visits (8.6%) is a patient who never completed care, and 8.6% of the Resources total is attributable to them. The overall conclusion -- that a large minority of ED attendances are labelled potentially avoidable, and that the three algorithms agree poorly -- is not overturned. But the Resources estimate is inflated by patients whose records are empty because they left, not because their need was low.

## Implications for the manuscript

1. The statement that Resources-flagged patients were "discharged home" is not accurate and should be replaced with an explicit statement of the exclusion list (ADMITTED, TRANSFER, EXPIRED) and of which dispositions remain eligible.
2. DAMA is identifiable (N = 756) and was included, as were LWBS (N = 2,597) and ELOPED (N = 1,913). This belongs in the methods.
3. The scenario-2 sensitivity analysis above is the natural addition: it is a one-line change to the rule and shows both the direction and the magnitude of the bias.
4. The construct-validity limitation should be named directly -- the Resources algorithm cannot distinguish "needed few resources" from "received no resources", so its flag is partly a marker of care interruption. This also bears on generalisability: the flag rate will track a department's LWBS rate, which varies widely with crowding.
5. Downstream utilisation of DAMA patients cannot be studied in this first-visit-only sample; that would need the full multi-visit file.
